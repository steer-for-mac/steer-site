Help guide

The in-app help page covers setup, button mapping, profiles, gyro, haptics, and troubleshooting. Open Steer → Preferences → Help, or hold Command and press ? from the menu bar.

When can I actually buy it?
Steer is in final testing. The launch list gets one email the day the free trial opens, and nothing before that. The trial runs 14 days with no card and no account, so you can decide with the app in your hands rather than from this page.
What permissions does it need, and why?
Accessibility, so Steer can move the cursor and type: the app can't do its job without it. Input Monitoring for three things: the DualSense Edge back buttons, tilt-to-aim on a DualShock 4, and off-brand pads macOS does not recognise on its own. No tracking, no analytics, and your setup stays on your Mac. The full picture is in the trust section and the privacy page.

Controller not detected

Connect via USB first to confirm the controller is working, then try Bluetooth. Use “Scan for controller” in the menu bar. If Steer was recently updated, macOS may have revoked Accessibility. Check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Input Monitoring is a separate permission and is not needed for detection: it applies only to a DualSense Edge's rear paddles, tilt-to-aim on a DualShock 4, and off-brand pads.

Why not just use a mouse and keyboard?
At a desk doing precise work, a mouse is better, and Steer is not trying to replace it there. Everywhere else is the point. Your hands stay on the controller: pointing, clicking, dragging, typing, and your shortcuts are all on one device, with nothing extra to buy or charge. Dragging is real dragging, so moving a file into a folder works the way it does with a mouse.
Is a stick actually precise enough for real work?
For clicking a menu item, hitting a link, dragging a file, yes, once the speed suits your hands: the cursor crawls when you nudge the stick and sweeps when you push it, and you set that yourself. For pixel work in Photoshop, no. Most people set the cursor speed once on the first day and stop thinking about it. This is the part you have to feel, which is what the trial is for.
