The keyboard, the mouse, and then some.

Three things a phone remote can’t do: every button already has a job, it types, and it learns your apps. Then the rest.

Pictures are the app with a DualSense; button names follow your controller.

{{ shot("help", 2078, 1500, "Steer’s help card: a DualSense with every control labelled. Cross is Left Click, the left stick moves the mouse, pressing the right stick opens the on-screen keyboard.") }}

It already works

Every button already has a job.

Plug it in and point, click, scroll and switch apps. On a DualSense, press Create, the small button left of the touchpad, and the help card shows what every button does in the app you’re using. Hold a shoulder button and the others take a second job until you let go, like Shift on a keyboard.

{{ shot("keyboard", 2354, 1248, "Steer’s on-screen keyboard: a full Mac layout split in two, with F and J lit where each stick is aiming.") }}

It types

A real keyboard, for two thumbs.

Each stick aims at its own half of a Mac keyboard and the triggers type. Space, Delete and Return sit on the face buttons. Or press the left stick and say it.

{{ shot("apps", 1714, 873, "Steer’s Gallery: ready-made setups for Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Lightroom Classic, drawing apps, Keynote, Terminal, Xcode and code editors.") }}

It learns your apps

Every app gets its own buttons.

Add Final Cut Pro, Lightroom Classic or Keynote from the Gallery, and its buttons switch in when you open the app and back when you leave.

{{ shot("ring", 704, 838, "Steer’s ring of apps: Safari, Finder, Music, Mail, Calendar, Notes, Messages, Settings and Maps, with Aim, Select and Cancel below.", "h-bare") }}

Your apps, in a ring.

Press the left stick, point, and you’re in the app. Build rings of your own for anything you do often.

{{ shot("snap", 1120, 965, "Steer’s Window Snap: nine places for a window, from Top Left to Bottom Right, with Centre picked.") }}

Windows where you want them.

Pick one of nine places on the screen and the window moves there.

{{ shot("tiles", 1817, 203, "Five settings tiles: Button Combos, Gyro, Lights, Trigger Resistance and Vibration.") }}

Feel it, then make it yours.

On a DualSense, the triggers push back as you press. Vibration answers when Steer does something, each set of jobs gets its own light colour, and you can tilt the controller to aim. Click any button on the picture in Settings to change what it does, and History keeps restore points so you can always go back.
