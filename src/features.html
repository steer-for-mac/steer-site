What else does it do? Everything you'd otherwise get up for.

It works the moment you plug a controller in. Everything below is there when you want it.

How do I switch apps? Press the left stick.

Your apps sit in a ring. Aim at one with the stick and press ✕. Hold L1 and press the left stick for a second ring: Screenshot, Lock Screen, Show Desktop and more. Both are yours to change.

Can it move my windows? Window Snap has nine places.

Hold L1 and press ○, aim at a place, press ✕. It works through the window apps Rectangle or Raycast if you have one, and through the Mac's own tiling if you don't (macOS 15 and later).

Will I know it worked without looking? It rumbles, lights up and stiffens the triggers.

A short buzz confirms what Steer just did. On PlayStation pads the light takes a colour for each layer, and on a DualSense the triggers can stiffen as you press.

What if I don't edit video? Drawing apps, Terminal, Xcode and code editors are set up too.

Each card gives a family of apps its own buttons. The Gallery also has rings for writing, Safari and the Mac itself, and blank rings for Keyboard Maestro and Hammerspoon.

What else is in there? The whole list.

Point and click

Move the pointer
The left stick. Set its speed, how fast it builds, and how far you push before it moves.
Click and drag
✕ clicks, ○ right-clicks, and holding ✕ drags.
Scroll
The right stick. A PlayStation touchpad can scroll or point instead.
Tilt to aim
Tilt the controller to steer the pointer, on pads with a gyro. On a DualShock 4 it's off by default and in beta.
Worn sticks
A stick that drifts is re-centred automatically.

Type

On-screen keyboard
Press the right stick. It suggests words as you go: R1 picks one, △ types it.
Dictation
Press the touchpad, or the left stick while the keyboard is open, and speak. The app you're using has to offer Dictation.
Any keystroke
Any button can send any key or combination.
Saved text
A button can type a phrase, an address or a snippet you use all the time.

Get around

App menus
Hold L1 and press Options to step through the menus of the app you're using, for commands with no shortcut.
The usual keys
Options opens Mission Control, R1 Spotlight, L2 the app switcher.
Media and volume
Hold L1: the D-pad sets the volume and skips tracks, R2 plays and pauses.
Laser pointer
A dot you move with the stick, for presenting to a room.

More from fewer buttons

Ways to press
One button can do one thing on a tap, another on a long press, another on a double tap, and repeat while held.
Layers
Hold L1, R1, L1 and R1, L2 or R2 and every other button changes job, the way Shift changes a keyboard. A layer can stay on so you're not holding anything.
Button Combos
Buttons pressed together, or one after another, can be their own command.
Macros
A recording of presses you play back from a button.
The touchpad
On PlayStation pads, a trackpad, a scroll surface, or zones that act as extra buttons.
Sticks with other jobs
A stick can scroll, press arrow keys, act as eight buttons, switch tabs or Spaces, or nudge a window.

Make it yours

Your apps
Each app can have its own buttons, and they change as you switch apps.
Overrides
Settings that apply on top of whichever app you're using. Pause overrides turns them all off for now.
Clash warnings
Steer warns when a button would send a shortcut the Mac already uses: the screenshot keys, Spotlight, the app switcher, Command-Q and Command-W.
Undo and History
Command-Z undoes an edit in Settings. History lists each change with where it came from, and can take you back to an earlier point.
Share a setup
Export yours as a file, or import someone else's. An import can point to a script you've already installed, but never brings its own.
Your language
32 languages.

Wire it up

Shortcuts and scripts
A button can run an Apple Shortcut or a script, or open a link.
Drive Steer from elsewhere
31 actions in Apple's Shortcuts app, and links other programs can open.
Coding agents
The controller can show and feel what an agent is doing. How that works

What it won't do

The login screen
Your password before the desktop loads is out of reach, and so is Touch ID.
Pixel work
Retouching and precise vector work need a mouse or a tablet.
Long writing
Use a keyboard. The on-screen one is for short entries.
Every controller equally
The controller page marks every gap, including pads nobody has confirmed yet.

Still wondering? The questions people ask.

Why not a wireless keyboard with a trackpad?
It still needs a lap or a table. A controller is held in two hands with nothing under it, and pointing, typing, your shortcuts and the menus are all under your thumbs.
Is this a subscription?
No. One payment, yours to keep, with free updates as macOS changes.
Does it lag?
No. Your presses never go over the internet: the controller talks to Steer, wired or over Bluetooth, and Steer sends the same input a mouse and keyboard do.
Does it drain the controller's battery?
Pointing and typing barely use any. Rumble and trigger resistance cost battery, which is part of why they are kept to a few moments or off.
Can I still use my mouse and keyboard?
Yes. Steer works alongside them; it doesn't replace them. Nothing needs switching.
Will I be able to read my screen from the sofa?
Steer can't make small text bigger; the Mac's own text size and zoom can. Steer handles the other half, reaching what you can already read.
Why not the Mac App Store?
The App Store can't offer a real, no-account free trial. You download Steer from the developer, and Apple still checks each release before it will run (notarization), so you get the safety check without a store in between.
Does Steer rely on undocumented behaviour?
Yes, and it's disclosed. Trigger resistance, PlayStation and Switch Pro rumble, and the light use each controller's own undocumented commands, worked out from scratch. The native pinch and rotate gestures recreate an event format Apple uses but hasn't documented. It's all built with Apple's published developer tools, and no private Apple code is linked in.
Can I get a refund?
Yes. There are refunds within 14 days, and a free trial before you pay at all.
