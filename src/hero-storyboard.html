Steer homepage storyboard

Storyboard v1

Frames, not comps. Grey blocks are shots that need a camera or a capture. Everything in quotes is proposed final copy.

  1. 01
    PHOTO A real room. Controller resting in the foreground, in hands or on a sofa arm. The Mac across the room, awake and lit. One graphic overlay only: the face button, a hairline, and the pointer it moved. Logitech's CMD+C / CTRL+V desk shot is the reference.

    Hero

    A Mac app for the controller you already own

    A mouse needs a desk.
    Your controller doesn't.

    Steer turns a game controller into a mouse and keyboard for your Mac. It works the moment you plug it in, and nothing you type ever leaves your computer.

    Join the launch list  ·  €19.99 once, when it ships  ·  macOS 14+

    Why: the teacher passes every word. The trust clause moves into the hero because the permission prompt is the biggest drop-off and it is the one line a competitor with analytics cannot copy.

  2. 02
    PHOTO ROW Six circles, real situations, not stock smiles. Logitech puts this at the very top as navigation. On the sofaPresentingEditingReading in bedCodingWhen a mouse is uncomfortable

    Where people use it

    Not a gaming thing.

    Six ordinary situations, each one a link into the use case.

    Why: breadth beat the single wedge twice on the record, so the page shows the set rather than picking one. This is also the fastest answer to the non-gamer's silent first question.

  3. 03
    BUILT Two columns: macOS on its own / With Steer. Already rendering in the gap band.

    The gap

    Your Mac can remap a controller.
    It cannot be run by one.

    macOS has a Game Controllers pane, and it does one job: swap a button for another button, inside games. Nothing in it points, types, or opens an app.

    Why: this is the story a blogger would actually run, which matters more than usual because the site is the entire funnel.

  4. 04
    EXISTING ART + NUMBERS The hero pad art, big, with six numbered dots. The legend sits underneath as a list. Replaces eight callouts set at 9px.
    1. Left stick moves the pointer
    2. Right stick scrolls
    3. Cross clicks
    4. R1 opens Spotlight
    5. L2 switches apps
    6. Options opens Mission Control

    What it does

    Every button already does something useful.

    Nothing to set up. Change any of it later, or don't.

    Why: Logitech numbers the device and lists the meanings below. It is legible at any size and it survives translation into 35 languages, which prose callouts at 9px do not. Every line above is a shipped default from Defaults.swift.

  5. 05
    CSS LOOP A .vg vignette: hold the shoulder button, four face labels flip to their second job. Per-pad by construction.

    Layers

    Hold one button and the rest change job.

    Like Shift on a keyboard. Five holds, so a handful of buttons covers a whole day.

    Why: this is the first thing that is not a mouse, and the first thing a €4.99 app cannot match.

  6. 06
    CARD STRIP Same pad, repeated, once per app, with the app's name and a short legend beneath each. Logitech's credibility engine. SafariFinal Cut ProDaVinci ResolveKeynote

    Per-app

    It knows which app you are in.

    The same buttons do different jobs in a browser, a video editor, and a slide deck.

    Why: named third-party software is how this category buys credibility, and the curb already requires each name to carry its category. Check every app named here is really shipped as a profile first.

  7. 07
    CSS LOOP The daisy wheel, mid-word, over a text field.

    Typing

    Type without a keyboard.

    Good for a search, an address, a short reply. Not for writing an essay.

    Why: the second sentence is the churn fix. The user who quit after a week quit because the first sentence promised keyboard speed.

  8. 08
    BUILT The Feel band, with the accent picker that already works.

    Feel

    The part you feel.

    The trigger stiffens before it clicks. A quiet buzz for the moments that earn it.

    Why: the only claim on the page that no screenshot and no competitor page can carry at all.

  9. 09
    BUILT Trust band, four cards.

    Trust

    Nothing you type ever leaves your Mac.

    No account. No tracking. No analytics, crash reporting or telemetry of any kind.

    Why: verbatim from the app's own Strings.swift. FIX FIRST: the live band says "no cloud" while the app ships optional iCloud profile sync, which is the overstatement the curb forbids.

  10. 10
    BUILT Pricing card with the inline launch-list form.

    Price

    One price. Yours to keep.

    €19.99 once, when it ships. 14-day trial, no card, no account.

    Why: unchanged. The single primary CTA in the page flow.

Off the storyboard, deliberately

The radial launcher: nobody has seen it, so it cannot teach itself in a hero. Logitech's equivalent, the Actions Ring, is one small card deep in their page. It belongs on the features page, not here. The compatibility checker is the top of the funnel and is a separate page, not a frame in this scroll.
